The Algorithms

Behind each tool lies a mathematical foundation. Here are some of the key techniques that power these experiments.

01

Simplex Noise

A gradient noise function that generates smooth, natural-looking randomness. Used in flow fields, terrain generation, and texture synthesis. Unlike value noise, it produces no visible grid artifacts.

Flow Field · Perlin Noise · DLA
02

Navier-Stokes Equations

The fundamental equations of fluid motion. Our solver uses Jos Stam's "Stable Fluids" method — a semi-Lagrangian approach that guarantees stability even at large time steps.

Fluid Simulation
03

Gray-Scott Model

A reaction-diffusion system where two chemicals interact and diffuse at different rates. The balance between reaction and diffusion creates spots, stripes, and labyrinthine patterns — the same math behind animal coat markings.

Reaction-Diffusion
04

Strange Attractors

Chaotic dynamical systems where trajectories never repeat but stay bounded within a fractal structure. Each attractor type (Lorenz, Clifford, Svensson) produces a unique signature shape from just a few equations.

Strange Attractors
05

Boid Flocking

Craig Reynolds' model of emergent group behavior. Three simple rules — separation, alignment, and cohesion — applied to each agent produce lifelike flocking without any central coordination.

Boids
06

Diffusion-Limited Aggregation

Particles perform random walks until they contact a growing structure, then stick. The result: branching, tree-like fractals that mimic snowflakes, coral, mineral deposits, and lightning.

DLA · Physarum
07

Voronoi Tessellation

A method of partitioning space into regions based on distance to seed points. Combined with Delaunay triangulation, it creates the dual graph that defines cell boundaries — used in everything from mesh generation to art.

Voronoi
08

Lindenmayer Systems

Formal grammars that rewrite strings of symbols according to production rules. Starting from an axiom, repeated rewriting generates complex fractal structures. Paired with turtle graphics, L-systems model botanical growth with striking fidelity.

L-Systems
09

Space Colonization

An algorithm where branch tips grow toward nearby attractor points within an influence radius. As branches consume attractors, they compete for space, producing organic tree-like and vein-like structures seen in real vascular systems.

Space Colonization
10

Slime Mold Simulation

Virtual organisms deposit chemical trails as they move, creating feedback loops between sensing and depositing. The emergent network patterns mirror real Physarum polycephalum — solving mazes, optimizing transport networks, and forming efficient spanning trees.

Physarum
